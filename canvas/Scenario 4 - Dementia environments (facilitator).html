Scenario 4: Dementia: Experience the Environment Differently

A familiar environment is altered with glare, patterned floors, noise and clutter. Students experience it, then redesign it using dementia-friendly principles. (about 70 minutes)

Learning outcomes

  1. Describe perceptual and cognitive changes that affect how people with dementia experience environments
  2. Identify environmental features that disorient or distress
  3. Apply dementia-friendly design principles to redesign a space
  4. Link environmental design to person-centred care
Content note: Loud or disorienting audio and flicker. Check for photosensitivity, sensory sensitivities or motion sickness. Students may have relatives living with dementia.
Scene preparation: Two versions of the scene are needed: (1) baseline, and (2) modified: harsh or uneven lighting and glare, low contrast (e.g. white toilet on white wall), patterned or shiny flooring, dark mats at thresholds, confusing or text-only signage, competing sounds (tannoy, trolleys, music, voices), clutter.

Stage 2 of 6 · 6 min

Modified environment

Room cue: 360° scene: modified version + layered audio

Same task. Find the milk, then the exit.

Questions (3), click to show

How did that feel?

What made it harder?

What did you do to cope?

Facilitator notes
  • Turn the audio up. Let students struggle a little before discussing.
  • Ask how they felt: frustrated, anxious, rushed, embarrassed?

Stage 3 of 6 · 10 min

Why is this hard?

Room cue: Modified scene (hold)

For someone living with dementia, these features can make everyday spaces confusing or frightening.

Questions (3), click to show

What might a person with dementia see or hear here?

How might that show up in their behaviour?

Could this be mistaken for being "difficult" or "aggressive"?

Reveal 1
Dark mats or thresholds may look like holes or steps.
Reveal 2
Shiny floors can look wet. Patterns can look like objects on the floor.
Reveal 3
Low contrast makes toilets, handrails and doors hard to pick out.
Reveal 4
Background noise can make it much harder to process speech and follow instructions.
Facilitator notes
  • Dementia can affect depth perception, contrast sensitivity, attention and memory for the layout of a place.
  • "Behaviour that challenges" is often communication of an unmet need or distress.

Stage 4 of 6 · 15 min

Redesign

Room cue: Modified scene (hold)

In groups, redesign this space so it is dementia-friendly.

Questions (5), click to show

Lighting

Flooring and contrast

Signage and wayfinding

Sound

Rest, reassurance and people

Reveal 1
Principles: even natural light, no glare · plain matt floors with a consistent tone · contrasting colours for key objects · signs with picture and words at eye level · less noise · places to sit · familiar cues
Reveal 2
Tools: The King's Fund EHE environmental assessment tools · University of Stirling DSDC design guidance
Facilitator notes
  • Staff are part of the environment: a calm approach, time, and a person-centred conversation.
  • Ask whether dementia-friendly design also helps other people (older people, people with visual impairment, autistic people). Usually it does.

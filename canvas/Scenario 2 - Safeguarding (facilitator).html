Scenario 2: Spot the Safeguarding Concerns

An immersive home full of clues. Students observe silently, then information is revealed gradually, moving from observation to safeguarding response. (about 75 minutes)

Learning outcomes

  1. Distinguish between an observation, a concern and an assumption
  2. Demonstrate professional curiosity through questioning
  3. Recognise indicators of self-neglect and financial abuse
  4. Outline a proportionate safeguarding response under the Care Act 2014
Content note: Abuse and neglect. Remind students about support services and that they can step out.
Scene preparation: Place these clues in the home scene without drawing attention to them: little or no food, unopened medication (dated), dust or dirt and rubbish, unpaid bills or red reminders, a cold house (heater off, blankets, visible breath), signs of self-neglect (same clothes, soiled items), a bank card or letters addressed to someone else, and mobility hazards (loose rug, trailing cable, no rails).

Stage 2 of 7 · 8 min

What did you notice?

Room cue: Same scene

Share what you noticed, then sort each item into one of three columns: Observation, Concern or Assumption.

Questions (3), click to show

What did you see?

Which of these are facts, and which are interpretations?

What else would you want to know?

Facilitator notes
  • Three columns on the board: OBSERVATION / CONCERN / ASSUMPTION.
  • Challenge interpretations: "How do you know that's his?" "Could there be another explanation?"
  • Clues to check they found: food, medication, bills, heating, self-neglect, someone else's post or card, trip hazards.

Stage 3 of 7 · 5 min

Information 1: health

Room cue: Same scene

You now learn more about the person who lives here.

Questions (2), click to show

Does this change how you read the environment?

What is the concern now?

Reveal 1
Arthur Hughes, 84, lives here. He has type 2 diabetes and heart failure.
Reveal 2
He has missed his last three GP appointments.
Reveal 3
His weight has dropped by 6 kg in four months.
Reveal 4
The unopened boxes are his diuretic and diabetes medication.
Facilitator notes
  • Move from observation to concern: there is a pattern, not a one-off.
  • Unopened medication and weight loss could mean self-neglect, or could mean someone is withholding them. Keep both possibilities open.

Stage 4 of 7 · 6 min

Information 2: who else?

Room cue: Same scene

A neighbour talks to you on the doorstep.

Questions (2), click to show

What does professional curiosity look like here?

What questions would you ask, and who would you ask them to?

Reveal 1
"His nephew moved in about six months ago. Things went downhill after that."
Reveal 2
"Arthur used to do his own shopping. Now the nephew has his bank card."
Reveal 3
The red bills are for gas and electricity. Arthur's pension goes into an account the nephew manages.
Reveal 4
The nephew isn't in when you visit. His room has a new TV and games console.
Facilitator notes
  • Possible financial or material abuse. Other types in the Care Act statutory guidance include neglect and acts of omission, psychological abuse, and self-neglect.
  • Professional curiosity means not taking things at face value, and "respectful uncertainty".
  • Avoid confirmation bias: the neighbour's account is information, not proof.

Stage 5 of 7 · 6 min

Information 3: Arthur's voice

Room cue: Same scene (optional: audio of Arthur)

You speak with Arthur alone.

Questions (3), click to show

What does Arthur want?

Does Arthur have capacity to make decisions about his money and care?

How do you balance his wishes with protecting him?

Reveal 1
"He's family. I don't want to cause any trouble for him."
Reveal 2
"I don't like to ask for money. He says it's all being sorted."
Reveal 3
He's clear and well-oriented, but tearful when asked about his late wife.
Facilitator notes
  • Making Safeguarding Personal: what outcomes does Arthur want?
  • Mental Capacity Act 2005: presume capacity. An unwise decision does not in itself mean someone lacks capacity. Capacity is specific to each decision.
  • Coercion and control can affect choices even when someone has capacity.

Stage 6 of 7 · 10 min

Safeguarding response

Room cue: Neutral scene

Decide what happens next.

Questions (4), click to show

Does this meet the criteria for a Section 42 enquiry?

Who do you inform, and how quickly?

What do you record, and how?

Which of the six safeguarding principles apply here?

Reveal 1
Section 42 criteria: the adult has care and support needs, is experiencing or at risk of abuse or neglect, and because of those needs cannot protect themselves.
Reveal 2
Six principles: Empowerment · Prevention · Proportionality · Protection · Partnership · Accountability
Facilitator notes
  • Response: discuss with the named safeguarding lead, refer to the local authority adult safeguarding team, record facts and direct quotes, and inform the GP.
  • Consider an immediate risk: is there food? Is there heating tonight?
  • Record factually: what you saw, what was said (verbatim), what you did, and when.

  • Public health framing: prevention, population-level awareness, and financial abuse of older people as an under-reported harm.

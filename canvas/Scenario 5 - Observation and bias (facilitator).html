Scenario 5: What Can You See? Home Edition

Students enter a 360° home knowing nothing. They record observations, then the case history reframes everything. (about 55 minutes)

Learning outcomes

  1. Record objective observations separately from interpretation
  2. Recognise how assumptions and bias shape judgements
  3. Reconsider evidence in light of new information
  4. Identify hidden carers as a public health group
Content note: Caring responsibilities. Some students may be, or have been, young carers.

Stage 1 of 6 · 1 min

Enter the home

Room cue: 360° scene: busy family home (washing, pill organiser, school bag, wheelchair ramp outside, takeaway boxes)

You have been asked to visit this home. You know nothing about who lives here.

Questions (1), click to show

Don't discuss yet. Just look.

Facilitator notes
  • Say nothing more about the person or the purpose of the visit.

Stage 2 of 6 · 8 min

Document

Room cue: Same scene

Individually, write down everything you notice.

Questions (3), click to show

What can you see?

Who do you think lives here?

What do you think their life is like?

Facilitator notes
  • Encourage detail: brands, dates, positions, smells implied, what is missing.

Stage 3 of 6 · 8 min

Share and sort

Room cue: Same scene

Share your notes. Sort them into facts and interpretations.

Questions (3), click to show

What did everyone notice?

What did only one person notice?

Which of your notes are judgements?

Facilitator notes
  • Collect "who lives here" guesses. Students often assume neglect, chaos or a lazy teenager.

Stage 4 of 6 · 5 min

Case history: part 1

Room cue: Same scene

Here is who lives here.

Questions (1), click to show

Look again. What do you see differently?

Reveal 1
Priya is 15. She lives with her mum, Anita, who has multiple sclerosis.
Reveal 2
Priya manages her mum's medication, the shopping and most of the housework.
Reveal 3
Her school has recorded her as persistently late.
Facilitator notes
  • The pill organiser, the ramp, the takeaways and the unfinished homework all look different now.

Stage 5 of 6 · 8 min

Reconsider

Room cue: Same scene

Go back through your original notes.

Questions (4), click to show

Which observations still stand?

Which interpretations were wrong?

What did you miss completely?

What might a professional visiting for Anita have missed about Priya?

Reveal 1
Priya hasn't told anyone at school. She worries that she and her mum would be separated.
Facilitator notes
  • Young carers are often hidden. The Children and Families Act 2014 gives young carers the right to an assessment of their needs.
  • Think Whole Family approach.

Stage 6 of 6 · 8 min

Debrief

Room cue: Neutral / title scene

What we see depends on what we expect.

Questions (3), click to show

Which of your assumptions came from your own experience?

How can we guard against bias in practice?

Why are hidden carers a public health issue?

Facilitator notes
  • Unconscious bias, the fundamental attribution error, and confirmation bias.
  • Tip: record observations first, and interpretations separately.

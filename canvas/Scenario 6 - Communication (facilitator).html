Scenario 6: Communication Challenge

Communication gets harder as the environment changes, from a quiet room to a busy ward to a crowded waiting room to a person's home, and each setting brings a new service user need. (about 75 minutes)

Learning outcomes

  1. Adapt communication to the needs of the individual and the setting
  2. Identify reasonable adjustments for people with communication needs
  3. Apply the Accessible Information Standard
  4. Use techniques such as teach-back to check understanding
Content note: Role play. Set ground rules: no mimicking impairments or accents.

Stage 1 of 5 · 10 min

Quiet consultation room

Room cue: 360° scene: calm consultation room

You need to explain a new medication to Mr Okafor, 70, who has a hearing impairment.

Questions (3), click to show

How would you prepare the room?

How would you adapt how you speak?

How would you check he has understood?

Reveal 1
He wears hearing aids, but the batteries are flat.
Reveal 2
He relies partly on lip-reading.
Facilitator notes
  • Face the person, with light on your face. Don't shout. Speak clearly at a normal pace. Rephrase rather than repeat.
  • Check hearing aids. Written information. Teach-back.
  • If someone uses BSL, book a qualified BSL interpreter.

Stage 2 of 5 · 10 min

Busy ward

Room cue: 360° scene: busy ward + ambient audio (alarms, voices)

You need to discuss discharge with Mrs Kowalczyk, 58, who speaks limited English.

Questions (3), click to show

What's the challenge here?

Who could help you communicate?

What shouldn't you do?

Reveal 1
Her 12-year-old granddaughter is visiting and offers to translate.
Facilitator notes
  • Use a professional interpreter (face to face, phone or video). Don't use family members, and especially not children.
  • Move to a quieter space if you can. Use plain language, avoid jargon, and use teach-back.

Stage 3 of 5 · 10 min

Crowded waiting room

Room cue: 360° scene: crowded waiting room + loud audio

You call Daniel, 24, who has a learning disability and is visibly anxious.

Questions (3), click to show

What do you notice about Daniel?

How would you adapt the environment?

What adjustments could have been made before today?

Reveal 1
Daniel has a hospital passport in his bag.
Reveal 2
The noise and waiting are making his anxiety worse.
Facilitator notes
  • Reasonable adjustments (Equality Act 2010): a quiet space, first or last appointment, longer appointments, easy read information.
  • Hospital passport. Involve his supporter, but speak to Daniel directly.
  • LeDeR (Learning from Lives and Deaths): people with a learning disability die younger, often from avoidable causes.

Stage 4 of 5 · 10 min

Person's home

Room cue: 360° scene: home environment

You visit Elsie, 86, who has cognitive impairment, to talk about her care plan.

Questions (3), click to show

How is communicating here different from the ward?

How would you support Elsie to take part in decisions?

Who else might you involve?

Reveal 1
Elsie repeats questions and becomes upset when she can't remember your name.
Facilitator notes
  • Short sentences, one idea at a time. Allow time. Use visual cues and reassurance.
  • Mental Capacity Act: support the person to make their own decisions as far as possible.

Reveal 1
Accessible Information Standard (2016): identify, record, flag, share and meet people's information and communication needs.
Facilitator notes
  • It applies to NHS and publicly funded adult social care providers.
  • Health literacy is a public health issue: poor communication widens inequalities.
